02About

A structural engineer building toward research

I am a Civil Engineering graduate of Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology (CUET). My focus is structural engineering, especially the seismic resilience of structures and sustainable infrastructure development, and I enjoy applying engineering knowledge to real-world problems.

I now work as a Structural Engineer at Engineering Alliance Inc., supporting structural design and analysis on live projects.

My undergraduate research asks three connected questions: how code-designed irregular buildings perform under earthquake ground motions, how alternative materials change the cyclic behaviour of RC members, and how hybrid finite-element and machine-learning models can predict the stiffness of low-cost seismic isolators. I am seeking a PhD position to take this work further.

Featured studyManuscript in preparation

Cyclic Performance of Reinforced Concrete Columns Incorporating Sandcrete Block Aggregate: Experimental and Numerical Study

Objective
Evaluate sandcrete blocks as a sustainable alternative to conventional bricks, and as a replacement for brick chips as aggregate in structural members.
Experiment
Two identical RC columns constructed and tested under reversed cyclic (push–pull) lateral loading.
Measured
Hysteretic behaviour, lateral strength and crack patterns.
Numerical
Numerical study of the column response alongside the tests.
Significance
Tests whether a more sustainable aggregate can be used in RC members that must resist earthquake loading.
Cyclic testingHysteresisSustainable materialsRC columns

Seismic Assessment of a Code-Designed Plan Irregular RC Building Using Nonlinear Time History Analysis

Objective
Assess the seismic performance of a plan-irregular RC building designed to code.
Method
Nonlinear time history analysis following BNBC 2020, using selected recorded ground motions.
Comparison
Results compared with equivalent static analysis through storey drift ratio and other response parameters.
Output
Paper presented at ICCESD 2026, Khulna University of Engineering & Technology.
NLTHAPlan irregularityBNBC 2020Storey drift
Manuscript in preparationFinite element & machine learning

Predictive Modeling of Horizontal Stiffness in Scrap Tire Isolators: A Hybrid FEM–ML Approach

Objective
Quantify how bonding condition, geometry, material parameters and loading conditions affect the horizontal stiffness of scrap tire rubber pad (STRP) isolators.
Method
Hybrid approach combining finite element modelling with machine learning.
Aim
Demonstrate that machine-learning models can accurately predict isolator horizontal stiffness.
